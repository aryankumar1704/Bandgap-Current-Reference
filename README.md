# Design of Bandgap Current Reference

Developed for the Department of Electronics and Electrical Engineering at IIT Guwahati under the guidance of Dr. Mahima Arrawatia, this project focuses on the design and simulation of a low-power Bandgap Current Reference[cite: 1]. The circuit achieves temperature independence by balancing a CTAT voltage ($V_{BE} \approx 0.7\text{ V}$), which decreases with temperature, against a PTAT voltage difference ($\Delta V_{BE} \approx 52\text{ mV}$ with an emitter area ratio of $n = 8$), which increases with temperature[cite: 1]. Using a supply voltage of $1.8\text{ V}$ and targeted branch currents of $5\ \mu\text{A}$, the network utilizes a calculated $140\text{ k}\Omega$ CTAT resistor and a $10.4\text{ k}\Omega$ PTAT resistor[cite: 1]. Under saturation constraints with an overdrive voltage of $0.15\text{ V}$, the transistor channel geometries are sized at $W/L \approx 6$ for NMOS and $W/L \approx 22$ for PMOS devices[cite: 1]. A PMOS cascode current mirror stabilizes the output against voltage fluctuations, while an integrated 3-transistor startup circuit prevents the architecture from getting trapped in a zero-current state during power-up[cite: 1]. Complete schematics and simulation parameters are documented in the accompanying bandgap_current_ref.pdf file.

DC operating point simulations in Cadence confirm that all core transistors are properly biased in the saturation region, with the startup circuit drawing negligible steady-state current once initialization is complete[cite: 1]. Parametric temperature sweeps from $-10^\circ\text{C}$ to $125^\circ\text{C}$ show that the output current varies tightly between $9.6\ \mu\text{A}$ and $11.4\ \mu\text{A}$[cite: 1]. The slight positive slope indicates that the PTAT component mildly dominates over the CTAT component, yet the output remains localized near the targeted $10\ \mu\text{A}$ baseline across the full thermal range[cite: 1]. With a total current consumption of $30\ \mu\text{A}$, the entire circuit operates with an ultra-low power dissipation of just $54\ \mu\text{W}$[cite: 1]. The design successfully fulfills its operational goals, delivering a reliable, highly stable baseline current block optimized for modern analog integrated systems[cite: 1].
